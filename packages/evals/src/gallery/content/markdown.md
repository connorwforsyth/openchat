# Country loaf: production method

The loaf every Copperloaf shop sells most of. This is the method the bake floor follows; the recipe card on the wall is the short version. Yield is **24 loaves at 950 g**, one batch in the 60-litre mixer.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Baker's % | Weight |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Bread flour (Laucke Wallaby) | 85% | 10.20 kg |
| Wholemeal flour | 15% | 1.80 kg |
| Water at 30 °C | 78% | 9.36 kg |
| Levain (100% hydration) | 20% | 2.40 kg |
| Fine sea salt | 2.2% | 0.26 kg |

Total dough is about **24.0 kg**, so each loaf scales at 950 g before baking and loses roughly 15% in the oven.

## Timeline

1. **Build the levain** (8 pm the night before). 1 part starter, 5 parts flour, 5 parts water. It should double and smell sweet by 7 am.
2. **Autolyse** (7:00). Mix the flours and 9 kg of the water for 3 minutes on slow. Rest 45 minutes.
3. **Mix** (7:45). Add levain and salt with the last of the water. 4 minutes slow, 3 minutes fast. Dough temperature should read **26 to 27 °C**.
4. **Bulk ferment** (8:00 to 12:00). Four sets of coil folds, 30 minutes apart, then leave it alone. Ready when it has risen by about half and shows bubbles at the edges.
5. **Divide and pre-shape** (12:00). 950 g pieces, loose rounds. Bench rest 25 minutes.
6. **Shape** (12:30). Tight bâtards into rice-floured bannetons, seam up.
7. **Retard** (13:00). Into the retarder at 3 °C for 14 to 16 hours.
8. **Bake** (5:00 next morning). Deck at 250 °C, steam for the first 12 minutes, then 26 minutes with the vents open. Internal temperature **98 °C**.

## What good looks like

- A deep mahogany crust with an ear along the score.
- An open, glossy crumb, with no dense band under the top crust.
- A clear hollow knock on the base.

## When it goes wrong

| What you see | Likely cause | Fix next batch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat loaves that spread | Bulk ran too long, or a warm retarder | Shorten bulk by 30 min; check the retarder log |
| Tight, dense crumb | Under-proofed | Give bulk another 30 min; check dough temperature |
| Pale crust | Oven not recovered between loads | Wait for the deck to read 250 °C before loading |
| Big holes under the crust | Shaped too loosely | Tighter shaping, more tension on the final roll |

> If two batches in a row go wrong, stop and call the head baker before the third. A wasted batch costs about $120 in flour and labour; a bad morning at the counter costs more.
